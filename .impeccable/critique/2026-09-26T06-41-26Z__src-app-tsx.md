## Design specificity verdict

PortfolioLens feels authored for investment research. Its editorial type, restrained forest palette, ledger details, risk comparison, and scenario flow fit the product. The largest missed opportunity is precision of explanation when numbers change meaning between views. The pages are related but have distinct compositions.

## Overall impression

The interface is visually coherent and confidently quiet. Its strongest moments turn portfolio math into an actionable insight. The next improvement is to make every comparison unambiguous: what period, what holding, and how long a change lasts.

## What's working

- The Overview's 24% capital versus 42% risk callout makes a complex metric tangible and links to deeper analysis.
- The Risk page leads naturally into a relevant scenario. Scenario presets provide a useful starting point.
- Sample-data labels and a guided-demo analyst set appropriate expectations; focusable chart controls and methodology access support exploration.

## Priority issues

1. **P1 · Applied allocations look permanent.** The final scenario confirmation says the update appears “throughout PortfolioLens,” but a reload restores the sample. State that it lasts **for this session** at confirmation and success. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
2. **P1 · Concentration comparison lacks context.** The “Less single-stock risk” preset moves NVDA from 24% to 14% and VTI from 18% to 28%. “Largest allocation” then rises to 28%, which can look like worsening concentration. Name the holding beside each value and distinguish a single stock from a broad fund. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
3. **P2 · Research combines time periods in one flow.** A 3M comparison chart sits directly above a table of “Past year” returns. Separate the one-year facts more clearly, or make the table follow the chart period. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.
4. **P2 · The correlation matrix repeats choices.** Its 36 focusable cells include the diagonal and mirrored pairs. A triangular matrix with noninteractive self-correlations would reduce visual search and keyboard travel. Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.
5. **P2 · Benchmark wording is too broad.** “U.S. market” represents the sample VTI series on a shared dollar scale. Label it “U.S. market proxy (VTI)” and explain the common starting value. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.

## Persona red flags

- **Jordan, first-time investor:** “Risk contribution” and “annualized volatility” appear before a plain next step; the first Overview heading does not state the task.
- **Alex, experienced investor:** Custom scenarios require manually balancing weights, cannot be saved or compared to another scenario, and disappear on reload.
- **Sam, keyboard user:** The 36-button correlation matrix is tedious to traverse, though the cells and chart sliders have accessible labels.

## Minor observations

- Mobile Research's horizontal asset strip needs a visible cue that more assets can be scrolled into view.
- The “Long-term portfolio” chevron suggests switching portfolios, but opens editing.
- “Sources & filings” carries an external-link icon despite opening an internal tab.
- Small muted metadata and dashed comparison lines merit a dedicated contrast audit.
- The scenario result's left accent border is a minor visual convention warning rather than the main design concern.

## Questions to consider

- Should concentration focus on individual stocks rather than the single largest holding?
- Could the first Overview screen tell a new investor, in one sentence, what to do after seeing the 42% risk finding?
